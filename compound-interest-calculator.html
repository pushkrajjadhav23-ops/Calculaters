How this is calculated

The nominal annual rate is converted to an effective monthly rate based on your chosen compounding frequency, then applied month by month alongside your recurring contribution.

Effective monthly rate = (1 + r/n)n/12 − 1

r = annual interest rate  ·  n = compounding periods per year

Each month: Balance = Balance × (1 + effective monthly rate) + Monthly contribution

The Split chart above shows the same result visually — how much of your final balance is money you actually put in (teal) versus growth from compounding (brass).

Frequently asked questions

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest already earned. Unlike simple interest, which only grows on the original amount, compound interest lets your balance grow on an accelerating curve over time.

Yes, but usually a modest one. More frequent compounding (daily versus annually) means interest starts earning its own interest sooner, which adds up over long timeframes, though the difference is typically a small percentage of total growth compared to the impact of the rate itself or how long the money is invested.

The Rule of 72 is a quick way to estimate how long it takes an investment to double: divide 72 by the annual interest rate. At 7% annual growth, for example, money roughly doubles every 72 ÷ 7, or about 10.3 years.

Time in the market is often the single biggest factor in compound growth, since more compounding periods let interest earn interest on itself longer. Starting a decade earlier with smaller contributions frequently outperforms starting later with larger ones, though contributing more helps at any starting point.
