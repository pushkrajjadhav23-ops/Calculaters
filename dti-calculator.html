How this is calculated

Both ratios divide monthly obligations by your gross (pre-tax) monthly income. The difference is what counts as "debt" in the numerator.

Front-end DTI = Housing payment ÷ Gross monthly income × 100

Back-end DTI = (Housing + Auto + Student + Cards + Other) ÷ Gross monthly income × 100

The Split chart above shows the same thing visually — how much of your gross income is already spoken for by debt payments, versus what's actually free to save, invest, or spend elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

DTI ratio is the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward debt payments. Lenders use it to judge how much additional debt you can reasonably take on.

Front-end DTI counts only your housing payment against your income. Back-end DTI counts all monthly debt payments — housing, auto, student loans, credit cards, and other obligations — against your income. Mortgage lenders typically look at both.

Most conventional lenders prefer a back-end DTI of 36% or below, with a front-end DTI of 28% or below. Some loan programs allow back-end ratios up to around 43-50% with compensating factors like a strong credit score or larger down payment.

You can lower DTI by paying down existing debt balances, avoiding new credit before a loan application, or increasing your income. Paying off smaller debts entirely (rather than just reducing balances) often helps more, since it removes a whole monthly payment from the calculation.
