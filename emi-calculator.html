How this is calculated

EMI uses the same fixed-rate amortization formula banks use for personal, auto, and home loans. Every installment is the same amount, but the split between principal and interest shifts each month as the outstanding balance shrinks.

EMI = P × [r(1+r)n] / [(1+r)n − 1]

P = principal loan amount  ·  r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)  ·  n = number of monthly installments

The teal region in the Roofline chart above is cumulative principal paid; the brass region is cumulative interest. The ridge line where they meet marks the point where each EMI starts putting more toward principal than interest.

Shortening the tenure or making prepayments toward principal reduces the total interest — even a small extra payment early in the loan can meaningfully cut what you pay overall.

Frequently asked questions

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment — a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender each month, covering both principal and interest, until the loan is fully repaid.

EMI is calculated using the fixed-rate amortization formula shown above, based on the loan amount, the monthly interest rate, and the total number of monthly installments over the loan tenure.

It depends on what you choose with your lender. Most let you either keep the EMI the same and shorten the tenure, or keep the tenure the same and reduce the EMI. Shortening the tenure usually saves more total interest.

Reducing balance interest, used by this calculator, charges interest only on the outstanding balance each month, so the interest portion shrinks over time. Flat rate interest charges on the original loan amount for the entire tenure, which usually works out more expensive for the borrower.
