How this is calculated

We calculate your standard monthly payment using the fixed-rate amortization formula, then simulate two payoff paths: one paying just that amount, and one paying your extra amount on top each month, tracking the declining balance until each reaches zero.

Standard payment: M = P × [r(1+r)n] / [(1+r)n − 1]

Each month with extra payments: New balance = Balance − (M + Extra − Balance × r)

The two Split charts above compare your total cost under each path — standard versus with extra payments. Watch the brass (interest) slice shrink on the right: that's the direct payoff of paying extra toward principal.

Frequently asked questions

Interest is charged on your outstanding balance each month. Extra payments go straight toward principal, so your balance drops faster, which means less interest accrues on every payment after that — the savings compound over the life of the loan.

Making extra payments as early and as consistently as possible saves the most interest, since balance reductions early in the loan compound over more remaining months. A lump sum applied early works similarly well to consistent smaller extra payments — what matters most is reducing principal sooner rather than later.

This depends on your loan's interest rate versus your expected investment return. If your loan rate is higher than what you'd reasonably expect to earn investing, paying extra toward the loan is often the safer, guaranteed return. If your loan rate is low, investing may grow your money more over time — though it carries market risk that a guaranteed interest reduction doesn't.
