How this is calculated

Your EMI is calculated with the standard fixed-rate amortization formula. The processing fee is a separate one-time charge, not part of the EMI, and the eligibility check compares your EMI to the monthly income you enter against the ranges lenders typically use.

EMI = P × [r(1+r)n] / [(1+r)n − 1]

P = loan amount  ·  r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)  ·  n = number of monthly installments (years × 12)

Lenders generally want your total EMI obligations under 40-55% of monthly income — enter your income above to see where you land.

Frequently asked questions

Most lenders prefer your total EMI obligations to stay under 40-50% of your monthly income. Above 55-60%, approval becomes harder and lenders may ask for a smaller loan amount, longer tenure, or a co-applicant.

A processing fee is a one-time charge lenders take to cover loan application costs, typically 0.5% to 2% of the loan amount. It's usually deducted upfront and is separate from your EMI payments.

Fixed rates stay the same for the loan term, giving predictable EMIs but usually starting slightly higher. Floating rates move with the market, often starting lower but changing over time. Floating rates tend to average out cheaper over long tenures, but fixed rates protect against rate increases.
